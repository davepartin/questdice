# Objective audit fixtures: Lone Wolf and Glass Shield (Claude, 25 October 2026)

Brief: the Director's checkpoint (reproduce Lone Wolf with one initial enemy and with a multi-enemy fight reduced to one; reproduce Glass Shield with the actual released shield faces and legal actions; report structural impossibility apart from low probability; no unmeasured threshold). **Nothing in the game changed.** Tables: [T22-objective-fixtures-measured.md](T22-objective-fixtures-measured.md); tool `node tools/t22-objectives.mjs`; the exact conditions are pinned by `test/objectivefixtures.test.mjs`. Bots on the real engine, released rules, 200 fights a cell; Act I step 2 goblins, two heroes (a Knight and a Bard). Small Act I monsters die quickly, so the forced-share figures are higher than they would be against Act III monsters; I have not measured that.

## Lone Wolf ("Never attack the monster a teammate attacks that round")

**Structurally impossible, proven both ways.** With one monster at the start, every fight had at least one round with two heroes striking the only monster alive (200 of 200), and the card passed 0 of 200 for any policy: it cannot be met. The check fails whenever both heroes strike the same monster in the same round; with one monster alive that is forced. The same happens in a bigger fight once it is reduced to one monster: even a hero who deliberately picks a monster no earlier teammate picked failed in 55% of two-monster fights, 81% of three-monster fights and 31% of four-monster fights, all because the remaining monster was the only target left for a round. The only fights that can pass are those where, every round both heroes strike, at least two monsters stand.

**Low probability, separately.** When no round was forced, a hero who tried passed 100% of the time (91 of 91, 38 of 38, 139 of 139), so the card is a legal action, not luck. The ordinary bot, which just attacks, passed 0 of 200 at every monster count; a human who does not know the rule will also fail. Only a hero who deliberately avoids the teammate can pass, and only 46% (two monsters), 19% (three) and 70% (four) of fights allow it. **What it costs the player when it fails:** nothing. A forced bribe pays the gold only if met and the team wins; failing loses nothing. So it is an unfair-looking card more than a harmful one.

**What this says about the Director's preferred remedy** (eligibility filtering): filtering by the starting monster count removes the certain case (one monster) but is not enough on its own, because a fight that starts with two or three small monsters is still forced in 55% to 81% of fights for a hero who tries. A filter that guarantees the card is satisfiable would have to be "at least four monsters", which are rare early. The alternative is a changed condition, which returns for review and is not applied: "never attack the monster a teammate attacks, in a round when two or more monsters stand". Under that wording a hero who tries passes in every fight (100% by construction), the ordinary bot still fails, and the card means something. I recommend that wording together with the existing deal (the card is only forced on a hero in a fight that begins with two or more monsters).

## Glass Shield ("Have no block at all in 2 rounds")

**Not structurally impossible**, even with a shield. Block comes only from blue faces and block powers, and a hero can legally throw a block face back with a free reroll. A "dodger" who rerolls every die paying block passed 84% of fights with a Round Shield and with a Tower Shield (Bronze d4), 67% with a Silver d6 Round Shield, and 68 to 71% with two daggers or Arrows and Bow. **Low probability for ordinary play:** with a shield and ordinary play the card passed 4% (Round Shield), 2% (Tower Shield) and 21% (Silver d6 Round Shield); two daggers and the Arrows pair give 51% and 32% (they have fewer blue faces, so the starters of other classes already meet it often). Owning a shield does not guarantee block (21% of Silver Round Shield fights still had two block-free rounds), but it makes the card rare and, for a dodger, costly: dodging lengthens the fight from 4.3 to 5.7 rounds. **My reading:** do not remove the card; the intent (drop your guard for gold) is what the player does when they throw block back. The remaining question for Astra is difficulty by loadout, and a card that is 2 to 4% for a starter shield and 50% for a no-shield hero is uneven: a filter that deals it only to a hero who holds no shield die, or counts "block of 3 or less" as the Crockpot audit proposed, would change that, but I did not measure a threshold and propose none.

**Not done.** Act II and III monsters, three or more heroes, human play, and the four other flagged cards. The eligibility filter and any changed condition return for Director review before release.
