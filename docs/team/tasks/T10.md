# T10: Share battle report (24 October 2026)

**Owner:** Implementation lead (Claude), branch `claude/battle-report`. **Status:** implemented, revised after the Director's review (see "Review of 24 October"), REVIEW. Not merged, not deployed.
**Asked by:** the Director, after Dave's playtest death (Act II, level 6, three monsters, a potion to about 75% health, about 5 block from a talent, then dead in the enemy attacks). Nothing recorded that fight, so nobody could say what the lethal blow was. This records the next one.

## What Dave sees

After any fight, on the victory or defeat screen, one small button under the normal actions: **Share battle report**, with one line: "Stored on this device only (the last three fights). Shared only when you choose." It opens a sheet listing the last three fights (tap to choose), a short preview, and **Share** (the phone's own share sheet, where there is one), **Copy** and **Download**. Return to camp / To camp stays the prominent button. Screenshots: [screens/](screens/) (390×664 and 375×548, plus the sheet after a failed write); a sample report: [sample_report.txt](sample_report.txt).

## What a report holds

Header: game build, mode (solo / shared phone / online table), difficulty, act, step, quest. Each hero: class, level, health, magic, potions and what each heals, weapons, powers and their levels, perks, charms, road effects (name and text, and the numbers the road gave). Each enemy: identity, health, power die, fight bonus, armour. Each round:
- **the moves shown before the player committed** (each monster's move and its damage range, as the plaque showed it);
- each hero's **health at round start, immediately before resolution, and after**; magic before and after;
- **potions** (who drank, for whom, health before and after, potions left), **powers** (which, what they rolled and gave, for whom), rerolls and heart nudges with their magic cost;
- the **final dice faces** (slot, sides, value), the **target**, and the **attack, block, pierce, heal totals**, with the engine's own breakdown (each weapon lane: face, plus, hand, talent, value);
- the engine's round report: initiative rolls and who went first, **each monster's rolled damage, the parts it was made of, armour, chill, block spent and damage taken** (per hero in a company), your strike and what it dealt;
- a `derived` block: **the blows in the order they landed, each hero's health after each, and the first one that took them to zero (the lethal blow)**.

Outcome and rounds. A `legend` says what is captured, what is derived and what is unavailable on this path.

**Captured vs derived.** Captured: written down by the game when it happened. Derived: arithmetic on captured numbers (healing lands first, then each blow in resolution order, minus the block left), always inside `derived` blocks and marked "(derived)" in the readable summary. Unavailable items are named in the report, never guessed.

## Where the data comes from (inspected before instrumenting)

`engine.resolve` and `resolveParty` already return a full round report (moves, rolled damage and its parts, block spent, damage taken, initiative order, health before and after, strikes with totals and lanes). That is the core of a report. Four things were not recorded anywhere: the moves as shown at the start of the round, health at the start of the round, potions drunk, and final dice before resolution. The engine already had `CAST_HOOK` listeners for powers and magic spending (used by the measuring tools). I added one more optional observer, `CAST_HOOK.trace`, called at five moments the fight already passes through: a round begins, a potion is drunk, resolution starts, resolution ends (before the next round begins). It is called with `?.`, receives live objects and copies what it needs, draws no random number, changes nothing and cannot throw into the game (the recorder catches its own errors).

## Storage and privacy

- `localStorage`, key `questdice.battlereports` (`questdice.lab.battlereports` under `?lab`). Newest first, **at most three**; the fourth replaces the oldest (tested).
- **Bounded, always:** one report is at most 40,000 characters of JSON. Over that: the dice sizes, then the per-weapon breakdowns and dice faces of the early rounds, then whole early rounds (down to the last one), then the detail inside the rounds kept, then equipment and enemy details, then everything but the last round's health and lethal blow, and last of all a short record of how the fight ended. Each step is listed in `omitted`, and the readable summary says "Left out: …". The result is never longer than the bound (for any bound of 1,000 characters or more).
- Full or blocked storage: it tries fewer reports, then keeps the latest in memory for this visit, never throws, never interrupts the game. Memory and storage are **one list, merged by report id and recency** (newest first, at most three), so a failed write never hides the newest fight behind older saved ones. If nothing could be kept, the share row does not appear.
- Built from a named list of fields. Never a password, token, save id, hash, salt or room code (tested with planted values and an online table with a room code and seat tokens). The hero's name is included.
- No Firebase write, no new authentication, no security-rule change, no cloud-save change, no analytics. Reports are not part of the hero save (checked in the browser: the saved hero holds no report).

## Measurements

Report size (JSON characters; the export adds the readable summary, about 30% more):

| Fight | Report |
|---|---|
| Solo, 1 round | about 4,700 |
| Solo, 2 / 3 / 4 rounds (three goblins, Act II) | about 7,700 / 10,800 / 13,500 (about 3,000 a round) |
| Solo, 30 rounds (an extreme) | 73,600 full; **38,900 stored** (21 rounds kept, 10 early rounds omitted) |
| Six heroes, 4 rounds | 51,000 full; **32,000 stored** (detail dropped, all four rounds kept) |
| Six heroes, 1 round, forced to 2,500 / 1,000 | within the bound at each (tested), with the outcome kept |

Worst case three reports at the bound: 120,000 characters, about 2% of a browser's usual 5 MB. In the browser, after four fights the key held about 12,000 characters in three reports, and survived a reload.

## Limitations (please read)

- **Not tried on a real iPhone.** Safari's share sheet with a file, its clipboard rules and its download behaviour are untested; the native share was exercised only through a stub.
- **Online is reduced** (table above), and was tested through the table code and its views in Node, not against Firestore or two real phones. The online victory screen's button was not rendered in a browser; the online defeat screen uses the same component as solo.
- A phone that misses an intermediate online update (the session keeps only the latest while a replay plays) cannot know the exact health before resolution. The report compares the table's command counter: the report is `exact` only when the round resolved on the very next command after the last view this phone saw; otherwise the health is labelled `last observed`, the order of health is not derived, and no lethal blow is named (who fell is still captured). This is conservative: an unseen command that changed nothing also makes it `last observed`.
- The derived order of blows follows the engine's documented order; if a rule changes there, this block must follow.
- Monster ranges shown online are computed from the public monster data the screen itself uses.
- Nothing is recorded retroactively: Dave's earlier death is gone.
- Long fights lose their earliest rounds (and say so); the export of a long fight can be about 50,000 characters, which some messaging apps cut: a file share avoids that.

## Review of 24 October (the Director's three points, and the toast)

1. **Memory fallback.** `saveReport` kept the newest report in memory when a write failed, but `loadReports` preferred any valid stored list, so the newest fight could sit hidden behind stale saved ones. `loadReports` now merges memory and storage by report id and recency (`savedMs`), newest first, at most three. The picker reads the same merged list through `pickerEntries`. Tested as asked (existing saved reports, then a new one whose write fails, then the picker's list from the same storage) and in the browser with writes to the reports key forced to fail.
2. **Hard size bound.** `fit` stopped removing rounds at two and could return more than the bound; it also measured the size before adding its own `trimmed` and `omitted` notes. It now measures with the notes in place, can drop to one round, strips detail inside what it keeps, and ends with a short outcome record, so the result never exceeds the bound. Tested for real one- and two-round six-hero reports and for padded reports.
3. **Online evidence.** The old code compared the observer's own potion count and health between views and wrote "drank a potion, healed N". If the potion was thrown to a friend, or a view was missed, that was wrong. Now only the belt count is taken from the view, as an event marked `inferred`, with recipient, healing and health `unavailable`. Pre-resolution health carries a basis: `exact` when the table's command counter shows the round resolved on the very next command after the last view this phone saw, otherwise `last observed`; in that case the order of health and the lethal blow are not derived (`derived.uncertain` says why), and who fell is still taken from the engine's own result. Tested for a thrown potion, a potion thrown to the observer, and skipped updates.

**Found while doing this (shared phone).** A potion thrown to a friend is drunk through the thrower's fighter object, not the battle, so the observer ignored it. Fighters are now registered with the fight, and the throw is recorded with thrower, recipient and healing (tested). My first PR text said potions were captured for the shared phone; that was true only for drinking.

**Also fixed.** With three reports the sheet's Close button was clipped at 390×664; the list and preview now scroll above pinned actions, and a script check asserts the button is in view at both sizes.

**Screenshots were not in the first PR.** The folder was named `shots/`, which the repository's `.gitignore` excludes (it is the dev scripts' scratch folder), so the files described in my first PR text were never committed. They are now in `screens/`.

### Why "Could not save. Is browser storage blocked?" appeared in the browser runs

It comes from `persist()` in `js/ui.js`: `SV.saveHero(hero)` returns false and the game toasts. `saveHero` only updates a save record that already exists for the hero's name; it returns false when there is none. The hero in my browser runs came straight from `E.newHero` and was never forged through the save flow (`SV.createSave`), so it had no record. Nothing was wrong with storage, and my change is not involved. `dev/savetoast.mjs` shows it, on a **main-based build with none of my code** and on this branch, with the same result:

| Hero | Toast | Save record afterwards |
|---|---|---|
| no save record (the harness) | "Could not save…" twice, on both builds | none |
| real record made with `createSave` | none, on both builds | updated (`saved` time later, `battles` 1), `saveHero` returns true |

Real players always have a record, because forging creates it. The screenshot script hides the toast only because of the harness hero.

## Real-iPhone result and two refinements (24 October)

Dave moved a complete report from the preview (build `20261024report3`) on a real iPhone. His hero Redwizpractice won Night Raid on Cinder Ford in two rounds, and the director found the JSON and the readable summary agreed with his screenshots. **Which route he used (Share or Copy) is not confirmed**, so neither is recorded here as tested on a real iPhone; the evidence below points to Share but does not prove it.

1. **Equipped but not in play.** The report listed Round Shield with the weapons although only the left weapon die (NW) rolled. A weapon can sit in a slot whose die is not unlocked at camp (the right weapon die costs 30 gold; `E.isActive` is the game's own rule). Each weapon now carries `inPlay`, and the summary reads, for example: `Sword (left weapon), Round Shield (right weapon: equipped but NOT IN PLAY, its die is not unlocked, so it rolled nothing)`. Nothing else about how the weapons are captured changed. Tested with a hero whose right weapon die is locked (no die rolled for it; labelled) and with the die unlocked (both in play, no label).
2. **The doubled heading.** Cause found in the code, not guessed: Copy puts `exportText` on the clipboard, which has the three-line heading once. Share with a file passed the file **and** `text`, and that `text` was exactly the first three lines of the summary (`QUEST DICE BATTLE REPORT`, the title, the build line). Apps such as Notes, Messages and mail clients insert the text and then the file's contents, so the same three lines appear twice. That matches what Dave pasted, so the transfer was most likely the share sheet, but a paste I have not seen cannot prove it. Fix: a file share now sends the file and a title and no `text`. The export format itself is unchanged (Copy and Download are untouched), and the text-only fallback (no file support) still sends the whole report once. Tested: the file share has no `text`, and the heading appears once in the file.

Not yet done: this fix is on the branch (stamp `20261024report4`); the published preview folder is still build `20261024report3` until the Director asks for it to be refreshed. No real-iPhone test of the new share payload yet.
