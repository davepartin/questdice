# T02: solo burst damage and sudden deaths (23 October 2026)

**Owner:** Claude (implementation lead). **Status:** REVIEW. Measurement only; no gameplay changed.

**Tool:** `tools/burst.mjs`, run with released rules.
- Solo campaigns, Acts I–III, all five classes, normal and hard, 4 independent campaigns each.
- **1,200 first-try fights**, retries counted as falls.
- Every round's damage is recorded as a share of maximum health.
- Every death in a round begun at **60% health or more** is replayed from the start of the fatal round, and from the round before, on 16 fresh rolls each, with three ways of playing:
  - **smart:** the bot used for all balance tables;
  - **wise:** looks ahead;
  - **guard:** drinks and heals early, then shapes the roll for block.

**Verdicts:**
- **unlucky:** the same play survives that round at least half the time.
- **avoidable:** some play survives at least 75% and the one used did not.
- **unavoidable:** no play survives 25%.

Tables: `docs/balance/exp_burst/burst.txt`; cases: `docs/balance/exp_burst/*.json`.

## The 36 sudden deaths

- **Unavoidable: 0. Unlucky: 29. Avoidable: 7.**
- On average, from the start of the fatal round, the same (smart) play survives 70% of the time; wise or guard play survives 88%. From the round before: 80% against 93%.
- **Where:** 29 of 36 in Act I. 34 of 36 against two or three attacking monsters. 11 in round 1, from full health.
- **Who:** the Wizard has 16 of 36 (24 starting health), including 4 of the 7 avoidable ones.
- **Readable?**
  - In all 36, the monsters' shown worst case (the top of each move's range, added up) was at least the hero's health at the start of the round. The danger was on the plaques, but only as several separate ranges.
  - 13 of 36 had a wind-up, breath, stalk or slam showing that round or the one before.
- **Preventable?**
  - 34 of 36 died with a potion still in the belt.
  - The simulated player only drinks below half health; a hero at 60–90% facing three attackers does not drink.
  - "Guard" play, drinking and healing first and then rolling for block, turns most of them into survivals.

Example (hard Bard, Act I step 2, round 2): from 60% of 30 health, facing a Cinderling (5–19), a Goblin Skulker (4–18) and a Magpie Gang (3–17), with 2 potions in the belt. It took 22 and fell. The same play survives 13% of the time; look-ahead play 75%; guard play 63%. That is avoidable.

## Reading

Dying from 60% is rare (about 3 per 100 fights) and never forced. It is concentrated in Act I, against two or three attackers, and on the Wizard. In almost every case the warning was technically on screen (each monster's range), but it has to be added up in the player's head. The remedy, a potion, was in the belt but used by a rule that waits too long.

## Proposals, separate and not applied (for Director review)

1. **Readable danger, no balance change.**
   - When the monsters' shown worst case this round reaches your current health, the health bar shows a small "Could fall" badge with the total ("up to 53").
   - Tap it for one sentence: block, drink a potion, or finish one first.
   - It uses only what is already on the plaques, and is a direct response to the "readable" criterion.
2. **Act I openings against three attackers.** Measure one of these, matched:
   - in Act I, a monster beyond the second does not attack in round 1 ("still getting up");
   - or Act I group fights roll the top monster's die one size smaller in round 1.

   No immunity threshold.
3. **The Wizard's opening health.** The hard Wizard has 3× the sudden deaths of other classes in Act I. The Barrier start (already −1.3 health a fight in Act II) or +4 starting health could be measured in Act I specifically.
4. **The simulator's potion rule** drinks only below half health, which is probably more cautious than real players. Repeat this audit with a "drink at 70% when three attack" rule before tuning numbers, so balance is not fitted to a bot habit.

None of these is implemented.
