## Direction and ownership
Dave is creator and final decision-maker. Director = ChatGPT: design, priorities, focused prototypes, evidence review. Implementation lead = Claude: production code, integration, tests and simulation batches. Art director = Grokbot: miniatures, consistent art and asset production. Story and Writing Lead = QuestStoryBot: story, campaign packs and player-facing copy drafts; code integration stays with the implementation lead. These role names are explicitly authorized by Dave.
Goal: a polished phone-based, middleweight cooperative board game for friends on a couch. Brief rules, meaningful choices, persistent heroes, readable danger, beautiful miniatures. Preserve magic's button-based interface and weapon tiles with hand choices and dice faces. Cards belong at suitable draw/reveal/keep moments.
Dave reports charms have been removed and perks revised: verify current code before proposing anything that restores old systems.
The live site is a development/playtest site. Small tested updates may be published for Dave to try. Preserve saves and make changes reversible. Published does not mean reviewed.
**Standing authorization (Dave, confirmed by Astra, 24 Oct 2026):** no one else is playing, so small, checked updates may be pushed to main frequently. Completed work within an approved task may be merged and deployed without asking again each time: check the relevant behavior, preserve saves, and record the commit or build, the verification and the known limitations on this board. **New gameplay decisions still need a ruling, and experimental balance changes stay isolated (a preview or behind a switch) until approved for live play.** Publish assignments, decisions and handoffs here promptly so they are not stranded on branches.
This board on main is the shared source for task IDs, owners, status and next actions. A link that names a branch points at evidence that is not merged yet. Use focused assignments with finish lines. Director spends effort on judgment, design and targeted checks; implementation lead handles bulk runs. Save evidence and concise summaries; do not rerun completed batches without a reason.

## Session protocol
How to work in the repository (commands, tests, stamp, screenshots, measuring tools, what not to touch): [WORKING_GUIDE.md](WORKING_GUIDE.md).
1. Pull latest state; read this board, latest PLAN handoff, and relevant files. Reconcile work already in progress before claiming a task.
2. One owner per task; record status and files/branch being edited. Do not edit another active owner's files. Refresh before writing shared docs; preserve others' changes.
3. Status: READY / IN PROGRESS / REVIEW / DONE / BLOCKED. Claim before editing; do not assume another conversation has received an assignment.
4. Return commit/build, files changed, tests, screenshots for visual work, limitations and next action. Use docs/team/tasks/<ID>.md for detailed evidence when needed.
5. Director reviews actual evidence; only mark DONE after required review. Human phone/playtest checks remain explicitly unverified until performed.
6. Board-based handoffs (Dave, 24 Oct): write updates, questions, review requests and decisions here, with detailed evidence in the task note. Address the next owner in the Next action column. Dave only needs to tell a bot "Check the team board"; do not ask him to copy messages between bots. Assistants do not automatically wake one another. Dave can prompt: "Read docs/team/BOARD.md and the latest docs/PLAN.md handoff. Continue your highest-priority ready assignment, record your result, and stop at its review checkpoint."
7. Keep prior branch/release conventions in PLAN in view; verify active branches before syncing. No force pushes or overwriting concurrent work.
8. Previews and merging. GitHub Pages serves `main` only, so **merging browser files at the site root to main deploys them to the live game; docs-only changes do not change gameplay.** A phone preview is a copy under `preview/<name>/` on main: it keeps its own saves (`questdice.preview.*`, shared by all previews on one phone), cannot reach the cloud, shows its build in a tag, and leaves the game at the site root untouched. Merging a preview-only PR is not a release. Remove a preview with `git rm -r preview/<name>`. Gameplay branches are never merged as part of a clean-up.
9. Board edits: this board on main is the one copy. If your branch also edits it, merge main first and keep main's table (add or update only your own rows); do not paste back an older table. Overlapping files: check `git diff origin/main...<branch> --stat` before merging and preserve both owners' work.
10. IDs: one ID, one task. Where two lists used the same ID, the later one gets a suffix and the old branch, PR and commit names are left alone (see T10 and T10-ART). A new ID is the next free number.
11. Merging completed work: under the standing authorization above, merge an approved task's finished work (behavior checked, saves preserved), stamp a new build when browser files change, then record the commit, build, verification and limitations in that task's row. Anything that is a new gameplay decision waits for a ruling.

## Review criteria
- Danger should be readable and offer meaningful responses. Death from 60% HP is not automatically a bug; untelegraphed or practically unavoidable routine bursts warrant investigation.
- Normal should reward reasonable play with recoverable mistakes; Hard should demand stronger resource use and coordination. These are design goals, not verified current behavior.
- Do not tune solely for high win rates. Check trivial wins, burst tails, choices that matter, battle length, individual contribution and real player pacing.
- Persistent progression must feel valuable without a veteran erasing teammates' turns or scaling newcomers into unavoidable deaths.
- Short rules: aim for one clear effect sentence plus necessary cost/duration/target. Details on demand; no concealed exceptions.
- Miniatures remain the visual focus. Exact die face can express rolled state; numbers expressing magnitude stay visible where needed. Avoid decorative duplicate data.
- Objective card and enemy-inspection screens need composed layouts, unobstructed actions and actual rendered screenshots. Enemy inspection should feature a larger miniature with compact adjacent stats.
- Simulations prepare for a group test; they cannot certify the fun or a percentage of readiness. Run a short two-person phone session before asking six friends for a full evening.
