# The arrows, first measurements (22 October 2026)

Stage 3 of the weapons experiment (off in the game; playable in the lab, `?lab`). Act II heroes of every class hold Arrows
(left) and the Bow (right), Bronze, against the four matchup fights, 16 fights a cell, wise and greedy bots
(`ARROWS=1 W2=1 node tools/matchup.mjs`; table: arrows_matchups.txt). Team games: `W2=1 AMMO=<kind> node tools/party.mjs 2 2`
(arrows_team_<kind>.txt). No monster pressure rules.

Health a fight costs, wise player (lower is better):

| | heavy (ogre) | swarm (3 goblins) | guard (armoured warden) | healer (oak) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal arrows + Bow | 9.4 | **49.1** (best arrow) | 16.1 | 7.8 |
| Piercing arrows + Bow | 7.3 | 53.6 | 19.7 (worst) | 9.0 |
| Fire arrows + Bow | 7.8 | 51.3 | 18.9 | 8.5 |
| Frost arrows + Bow | **5.9** (best arrow) | 51.1 | **15.3** (best arrow) | **6.9** (best arrow) |
| Sword + Shield (for comparison) | 4.6 | 40.3 | 8.6 | 4.5 |
| Dagger + Sword (all attack) | 8.0 | 47.3 | 14.2 | 8.6 |

What it says:

1. **Frost is the best arrow in three fights of four**, because halving a big blow is worth more than any number. Normal is
   the best arrow against the swarm, so Normal is already a real choice there, which ChatGPT asked for.
2. **Piercing is the worst arrow against the armoured monster**, the one fight it should win. The reason is in the faces: an
   arrowhead is a blank (the number 0) that pays the hand's Strength in its own way. On a d6, Normal arrows carry 4, 5 and 6
   where the special arrows carry two arrowheads and a 3, so a Piercing arrowhead gives up about 5 attack to turn the hand's
   Strength (often 2 to 4) into pierce. The warden's armour stops about 5 a round, so the trade loses. Fire has the same
   problem: half the Strength splashed is less than the 5 it gave up, even against three goblins.
3. **The archer is an attacking pair.** Every arrow sits near Dagger + Sword (all attack) and well behind Sword + Shield, and
   good thinking is worth less with arrows (+10 to +19 health a fight) than with Sword + Shield (+15 to +25). The Bow's guard
   is small by design.
4. **Team games**: the ammunition barely changes how a team does (the Ranger's lowest health 50-54%, its friend's 45-56%,
   all within the noise of 20 companies). Frost does not stack between two archers (tested).

The smallest fix to try next (still provisional, numbers only, the same pattern for all three special arrows as agreed): let an
arrowhead keep a modest printed number as well as its effect, so it gives up less than it gains. For a d6, special arrows
`R0 R1 R2 R3 A2 A3` instead of `R0 R1 R2 R3 A A` (the arrowhead hits for its number + Strength, and adds its effect).
Normal keeps its bigger 4, 5 and 6. Then measure again: Piercing should win the armoured fight, Fire the swarm, Frost the big
hitter, and Normal should stay best when you do not know what is coming.

## Second try: arrowheads that keep a number (ARROW_NUM=1; arrows_numbered.txt)

Special arrows `R0 R1 R2 R3 A2 A3` on a d6 (the same pattern for all three), Normal unchanged.

| | heavy | swarm | guard | healer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 9.4 | 49.1 | 16.1 | 7.8 |
| Piercing | 8.6 | 49.4 | 17.7 | 8.8 |
| Fire | 9.6 | 47.8 | 17.5 | 8.9 |
| Frost | **5.0** | **45.8** | **9.9** | **4.5** |

The numbers helped every special arrow, but **Frost now wins all four fights**, by a lot (the guard fight 9.9 against 16-18),
and Piercing still loses to Normal against the armoured monster. Halving a blow is simply worth more than any of the other
effects, and turning only the hand's Strength (2 to 4) into pierce is too little to beat 2d4 armour.

Next test (queued, ARROW_V2=1 with the numbered faces): **Piercing makes the whole arrow hit pierce** (number + Strength), and
**Frost takes the hand's Strength off the next blow** instead of halving it. Both then pay "the hand's Strength" in their own
way, like Fire, which is easier to explain on the card, and neither scales with how hard the monster hits.
