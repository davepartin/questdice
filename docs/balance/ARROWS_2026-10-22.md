# The arrows, first measurements (22 October 2026)

Stage 3 of the weapons experiment (off in the game; playable in the lab, `?lab`). Act II heroes of every class hold Arrows
(left) and the Bow (right), Bronze, against the four matchup fights, 16 fights a cell, wise and greedy bots
(`ARROWS=1 W2=1 node tools/matchup.mjs`; table: arrows_matchups.txt). Team games: `W2=1 AMMO=<kind> node tools/party.mjs 2 2`
(arrows_team_<kind>.txt). No monster pressure rules.

Health a fight costs, wise player (lower is better):

| | heavy (ogre) | swarm (3 goblins) | guard (armoured warden) | healer (oak) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal arrows + Bow | 9.4 | **49.1** (best arrow) | 16.1 | 7.8 |
| Piercing arrows + Bow | 7.3 | 53.6 | 19.7 (worst) | 9.0 |
| Fire arrows + Bow | 7.8 | 51.3 | 18.9 | 8.5 |
| Frost arrows + Bow | **5.9** (best arrow) | 51.1 | **15.3** (best arrow) | **6.9** (best arrow) |
| Sword + Shield (for comparison) | 4.6 | 40.3 | 8.6 | 4.5 |
| Dagger + Sword (all attack) | 8.0 | 47.3 | 14.2 | 8.6 |

What it says:

1. **Frost is the best arrow in three fights of four**, because halving a big blow is worth more than any number. Normal is
   the best arrow against the swarm, so Normal is already a real choice there, which ChatGPT asked for.
2. **Piercing is the worst arrow against the armoured monster**, the one fight it should win. The reason is in the faces: an
   arrowhead is a blank (the number 0) that pays the hand's Strength in its own way. On a d6, Normal arrows carry 4, 5 and 6
   where the special arrows carry two arrowheads and a 3, so a Piercing arrowhead gives up about 5 attack to turn the hand's
   Strength (often 2 to 4) into pierce. The warden's armour stops about 5 a round, so the trade loses. Fire has the same
   problem: half the Strength splashed is less than the 5 it gave up, even against three goblins.
3. **The archer is an attacking pair.** Every arrow sits near Dagger + Sword (all attack) and well behind Sword + Shield, and
   good thinking is worth less with arrows (+10 to +19 health a fight) than with Sword + Shield (+15 to +25). The Bow's guard
   is small by design.
4. **Team games**: the ammunition barely changes how a team does (the Ranger's lowest health 50-54%, its friend's 45-56%,
   all within the noise of 20 companies). Frost does not stack between two archers (tested).

The smallest fix to try next (still provisional, numbers only, the same pattern for all three special arrows as agreed): let an
arrowhead keep a modest printed number as well as its effect, so it gives up less than it gains. For a d6, special arrows
`R0 R1 R2 R3 A2 A3` instead of `R0 R1 R2 R3 A A` (the arrowhead hits for its number + Strength, and adds its effect).
Normal keeps its bigger 4, 5 and 6. Then measure again: Piercing should win the armoured fight, Fire the swarm, Frost the big
hitter, and Normal should stay best when you do not know what is coming.

## Second try: arrowheads that keep a number (ARROW_NUM=1; arrows_numbered.txt)

Special arrows `R0 R1 R2 R3 A2 A3` on a d6 (the same pattern for all three), Normal unchanged.

| | heavy | swarm | guard | healer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 9.4 | 49.1 | 16.1 | 7.8 |
| Piercing | 8.6 | 49.4 | 17.7 | 8.8 |
| Fire | 9.6 | 47.8 | 17.5 | 8.9 |
| Frost | **5.0** | **45.8** | **9.9** | **4.5** |

The numbers helped every special arrow, but **Frost now wins all four fights**, by a lot (the guard fight 9.9 against 16-18),
and Piercing still loses to Normal against the armoured monster. Halving a blow is simply worth more than any of the other
effects, and turning only the hand's Strength (2 to 4) into pierce is too little to beat 2d4 armour.

Next test (queued, ARROW_V2=1 with the numbered faces): **Piercing makes the whole arrow hit pierce** (number + Strength), and
**Frost takes the hand's Strength off the next blow** instead of halving it. Both then pay "the hand's Strength" in their own
way, like Fire, which is easier to explain on the card, and neither scales with how hard the monster hits.

## Third try: every arrowhead pays the hand's Strength its own way (ARROW_NUM=1 ARROW_V2=1; arrows_v2.txt)

Numbered arrowheads, plus: Piercing turns the whole arrow hit into pierce; Frost makes the target's next blow the hand's
Strength softer (instead of halving it); Fire unchanged (half the Strength, rounded down, to every other monster).

| | heavy | swarm | guard | healer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 9.4 | 49.1 | 16.1 | 7.8 |
| Piercing | 8.7 | 48.6 | **16.0** | 9.9 |
| Fire | 9.6 | 47.8 | 17.5 | 8.9 |
| Frost | **7.4** | **47.2** | **13.1** | **6.3** |

Much closer: the four arrows now sit within about 2 to 3 health of each other in every fight (they were 6 to 8 apart), and
Frost no longer runs away with it. Frost is still a little ahead everywhere, for the same reason two shields lead the weapon
tables: with no monster pressure, softening a blow is worth more than any extra damage. Piercing now ties Normal against the
armoured monster but does not beat it (arrowheads are only 2 of 6 faces, so the armour fight is mostly decided by the other
four). The last check is these same arrows with fury from round 4 (below).

Provisional recommendation: these rules (numbered arrowheads; Piercing = the whole hit pierces; Frost = next blow the hand's
Strength softer; Fire = half the Strength to the others) are the easiest to explain on a card and the most even so far.

## Reconciling the arrow formulas (23 October 2026)

The earlier agreed proposal and the later tested variants differ. Nothing below is settled until Dave chooses; all of it is
behind `EXPERIMENT.weapons2` (the lab) and the test switches in `WEAPONS2` (js/data.js).

**Shared ground (unchanged in every variant)**
- "Strength" for an arrow is the **arrow hand's rolled die** (the printed number on the left hand die), read before anything
  lifts it: the Heart Boost colour bonus (+4 on a red or blue weapon lane from heart 5 or 6) and a 2x Strength talent face do not
  change it. (Keen Edge and Ironward add to the lane's hit, not to Strength.)
- The ammunition is chosen at camp, free, for the whole quiver. The three special arrows share one face pattern; Normal has its
  own pattern with bigger numbers and no arrowheads.
- Effects happen right after the archer's own strike. Splash and the main hit both go through guard. Frost never stacks (two
  archers on one monster: one chill; in the Strength variant, the larger amount) and clears after one damaging blow.
- Frost timing: it softens the target's **next damaging blow**. If the monster acts after the archer this round, that is this
  round's blow; if it already acted, it is next round's.

**The two versions side by side**

| | Earlier proposal (agreed 22 Oct) | Later tested variant (Claude, 22-23 Oct) | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrowhead face | symbol only, number 0: hits for Strength | keeps a small number too: hits for number + Strength (d6: `R0 R1 R2 R3 A2 A3`) | `numbered` faces (`ARROW_NUM=1`) |
| Piercing | the Strength part becomes pierce | the **whole** arrow hit (number + Strength + Keen Edge) becomes pierce | `arrowRules.pierce: 'strength' / 'all'` |
| Fire | hits; **half** the Strength, rounded down, to every other monster; guard stops it | being tested: the **whole** Strength to every other monster; guard still stops it | `arrowRules.fire: 'half' / 'full'` |
| Frost | hits; the target's next damaging blow is **halved** | hits; the target's next damaging blow is **the hand's Strength softer** | `arrowRules.frost: 'half' / 'strength'` |

**Why the later variant was tried.** Measured (first try, above): with symbol-only arrowheads every special arrow gave up the
die's top numbers (4, 5, 6 on a d6) for an effect worth less, so Piercing lost to Normal against armour and Fire never won the
swarm; Frost's "halve" scaled with the monster's blow and won everything. Numbered arrowheads close the first gap; "the whole
hit pierces" makes Piercing the armour answer; "Strength softer" makes Frost pay the same currency as the others and stop
scaling with how hard the monster hits. With fury from round 4 these gave: Normal best against the swarm, Piercing beats
Normal against armour, Frost best against big hitters, Fire never best (hence the full-Strength test now).

**Decisions for Dave (for the final phone-play version)**
1. Arrowheads: symbol only (simplest to read) or a small number as well (more even)?
2. Piercing: Strength only, or the whole arrow hit?
3. Frost: halve the next blow (dramatic, scales with big hitters) or soften it by the hand's Strength (even, easy to predict)?
4. Fire: half or full Strength splash (result of the test below).

## Fire: half or full Strength splash (23 October 2026; arrows_fire_half.txt, arrows_fire_full.txt, arrows_fire_team*.txt)

Same as the fury run above (numbered arrowheads, Piercing = whole hit, Frost = Strength softer, fury from round 4), only the
splash changes. Guard still stops the splash, monster by monster (at a table, each monster's guard is shared by everyone who
hits it). Strength = the arrow hand's rolled die, as above. 144 fights a cell (16 per class per snapshot), wise player,
health a fight ± standard error:

| | heavy (ogre) | 3 goblins | 4 goblins | summoner (Bone Warden) | guard (armoured warden) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | 11.6±1.2 | 47.3±2.5 | 70.1±2.0 | 39.0±2.8 | 22.6±1.9 |
| Piercing | 9.8±1.1 | 47.9±2.5 | 72.2±1.9 | 37.8±2.5 | 20.4±1.6 |
| Fire, half | 9.4±1.1 | 48.4±2.4 | 71.5±1.8 | 39.9±2.5 | 21.6±2.0 |
| **Fire, full** | 9.4±1.1 | **47.1±2.4** | **69.9±2.0** | 39.1±2.5 | 21.6±2.0 |
| Frost | **7.7±1.0** | 47.7±2.4 | 71.1±1.9 | **36.3±2.5** | **17.0±1.6** |

Wins in the crowd fights: 3 goblins 74-79%, 4 goblins 42-44% for every arrow (4 goblins with fury is a very hard Act II fight).

Team games, two or three archers (`PAIRS=ranger,ranger;...`, all on Fire arrows, 12 companies x 3 runs, Acts I-II):

| | Ranger's lowest health · fell · damage taken a fight | friend's lowest · fell |
|---|---|---|
| pairs (2 rangers, or with a knight or wizard), half | 49% · 9% · 40.1 | 47% · 10% |
| the same, full | 51% · 7% · 35.2 | 51% · 8% |
| trios (3 rangers, or 2 rangers + knight), half | 54% · 6% · 33.2 | 54% · 6% |
| the same, full | 52% · 6% · 34.4 | 53% · 6% |

What it says:
1. **Full Strength makes Fire the best arrow against crowds, but only just** (about 0.2 to 1.6 health a fight better than
   half, well inside the ± range). Against 3 or 4 goblins all four arrows are within the noise of each other: the choice of
   arrow matters far less there than how the fight goes.
2. **Frost stays the clear answer to big hitters, armour and the summoner** (gaps of 2 to 5 health, larger than the error).
3. **Several archers do not run away with it.** Two archers with full Fire took about 5 less damage a fight; three archers
   changed nothing measurable. Guard soaking the splash keeps it in check.
4. Fire's effect is small because only 2 of 6 faces are arrowheads and the splash (1 to 6) is soaked by each monster's guard.

Recommendation: **full Strength, guard still applies** (it is easier to say, it is never worse in these tables, and it does
not scale out of hand), with the honest note that Fire's crowd role is modest; whether it feels worth choosing is a question for
the lab on a phone. Status: `arrowRules.fire: 'full'` is a switch; not yet on in the lab (see the lab list).
